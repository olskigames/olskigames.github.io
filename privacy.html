Moth: Dodge the Rain

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

The short version. Moth has no accounts and no sign-in, and I never see who you are. Your progress is saved only on your phone. The game shows ads from Google AdMob, and Google uses some information about your device to show those ads. In the UK and Europe you're asked first, and you can change your answer at any time in the game's Settings.

Who I am

Moth: Dodge the Rain ("Moth", "the game") is made by Olski Games, an independent developer based in the United Kingdom. For UK data protection law I am the "controller" for the game. You can contact me at olskigames@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

The game saves your progress on your own device: your light, best scores, medals, upgrades, looks, goals and settings (sound, music, vibration). This file never leaves your phone and I can't see it. It's removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall the game.

If you tap Share after a run, the game draws a picture of your score on your phone and opens Android's normal share menu. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose an app to share it with, and then that app's own privacy policy applies.

The game asks for one permission, vibration, for small buzzes during play. You can turn these off in Settings.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Moth is free and is paid for by ads from Google AdMob: a full-screen ad now and then between runs, and optional ads you can choose to watch for a reward, such as continuing a run. These ads are provided by Google LLC and Google Ireland Limited, not by me.

To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google's ads software in the game may collect and use:

  • your device's advertising ID;
  • your IP address, which can show your approximate location (country or city);
  • device information, such as model, operating system, language and screen size;
  • how you interact with the ads, such as whether an ad was shown, tapped or watched to the end;
  • diagnostic information, such as how the ads software is performing and any crashes it has.

I don't receive any of this information about you personally. AdMob only gives me totals, such as how many ads were shown in a day. Google explains how it uses information from apps that show its ads at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites, and its privacy policy is at policies.google.com/privacy.

Your choices about ads

  • In the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, the game asks for your consent (using Google's consent message) before ads are personalised. If you say no, Google shows ads that are not based on your interests, but it still uses some information for things like limiting how often an ad appears and preventing fraud.
  • You can change your choice at any time in the game: Settings, then Ad choices.
  • On any Android phone, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Settings, then Privacy or Google, then Ads). This stops ads being linked to the old ID.

Purchases

If the game offers an in-app purchase (such as removing ads), it is handled entirely by Google Play. I never see your payment details. Google tells the game only that the purchase was made, so it can unlock it on your phone and bring it back if you reinstall.

Google Play

When you download the game from Google Play, Google handles that under its own privacy policy. If you've allowed it in your phone's settings, Google Play may also send me anonymous crash reports and performance figures (such as how often the game crashes on a type of phone), which I use only to fix problems. These do not identify you.

The legal bases I rely on

Under UK and EU data protection law:

  • personalised ads are shown only with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings;
  • non-personalised ads, fraud prevention and keeping the game working rely on legitimate interests: keeping a free game running and safe.

How long information is kept

Your save file stays on your phone until you clear the app's data or uninstall the game. Information collected by Google for ads is kept according to Google's own retention rules, described at policies.google.com/technologies/retention.

Transfers outside the UK

Google may process ads information in other countries, including the United States. Google uses legal safeguards for these transfers, such as the UK International Data Transfer Addendum and the EU Standard Contractual Clauses.

Children

Moth is not directed at children under 13, and I don't knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has been shown personalised ads through the game, please contact me and I'll help.

Your rights

You have the right to access, correct or delete your personal information, to object to or limit how it's used, and to withdraw consent. Because I don't hold any information that identifies you, the quickest ways to act on these are:

  • change your ad choices in the game's Settings;
  • reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings;
  • uninstall the game, which deletes its save file;
  • contact Google about the information its ads software holds, through myaccount.google.com or its privacy policy.

You can also email me at olskigames@gmail.com. If you're unhappy with how your information is handled, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, or to the data protection authority where you live.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts using information differently, I'll update this page and change the date at the top before the change takes effect.
